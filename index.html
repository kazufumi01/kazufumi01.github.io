Free VST3 / AU for your DAW

macOS 11+ (Apple Silicon / Intel) is tested in Ableton Live. Windows 10 / 11 builds are made automatically but are untested on real hardware. Each download page has install steps in English and Japanese.

VST3 / AU plug-in Free

EQ Anatomy Lab 2 1.0 — plug-in

Play with this EQ and you will know which EQ you actually need. Nine analog EQ models with their own panels and circuits, and one button that lays any of them out as ordinary bands, so you can see what a classic EQ is really made of. Plus zero latency, analog and linear phase, dynamic EQ, M/S, transient/body and a resonance suppressor, with every display and meter explained where you point.

  • Models based on the Pultec EQP-1A, Neve 1073, SSL 4000, API 550A, Manley Massive Passive, Maag EQ4 and more
  • Analog EQ models expand into up to 24 bands you can still drive from the panel
  • Reference curve C: drop any audio file to see its average balance
  • Presets by use and by analog type, 15 skins
  • macOS 11+ (Apple Silicon / Intel); Windows 10 / 11 builds but is untested on real hardware
Get it free →

VST3 / AU plug-in Free

Compressor Anatomy Lab 2 β — plug-in

Build a compressor from seven blocks and hear why a FET, an opto, a vari-mu and a VCA sound different. Twenty-one models with hardware-style panels, an IDEAL switch that isolates each unit's character, 26 lessons and ear training.

  • Models based on the 1176, LA-2A, Fairchild 670, SSL bus compressor, dbx 160, API 2500, Distressor, Neve 2254 and more
  • Gain reduction, spectrum, harmonics, sidechain filter and loudness (LUFS, true peak, PSR / PLR) on screen
  • Limiter, clipper, spectral, upward, OTT and gain riding for comparison
  • macOS 11+ (Apple Silicon / Intel); Windows 10 / 11 builds but is untested on real hardware
Get it free →

Dynamics · Study note New

Compressor Study Note

A remake of the Compressor Anatomy Lab. Move a control and six graphs move together with a plain-language reading of what the sound is doing — so the effect is readable even with the sound off, in class or on the train.

  • On desktop, every graph, control and note fits on one screen; on a phone it reads as a scrolling notebook
  • Single-hit zoom, peak vs average, and dashed “before” overlays
  • Overlay VCA, FET, opto and vari-mu, FF/FB or Peak/RMS with the same settings

Chapter III

The original labs

The first browser versions

The first browser versions of the labs. They stay at the same addresses after the remakes.

Dynamics

Compressor Anatomy Lab

Threshold, ratio, knee, attack, release, peak vs RMS detection, feed-forward vs feedback — each one taken apart and explained.

  • Gain-reduction timeline and live transfer curve
  • Clickable signal-flow diagram
  • Presets from vocal to NY parallel
Open the lab →

Saturation

Saturator Anatomy Lab

What do “warm” and “thick” actually mean? Six circuits — tape, tube, transformer, transistor, diode, hard clip — shown as harmonics.

  • Even / odd harmonic bars from a test tone
  • Aliasing you can see, and oversampling that fixes it
  • Delta listen: hear only what the circuit adds
Open the lab →

EQ · Phase

EQ Anatomy Lab

One EQ curve, three ways to build it — zero latency, analog-matched and linear phase — and what each does to phase, timing and transients.

  • Phase and group delay overlaid on the EQ curve
  • Impulse response with pre-ringing marked in red
  • Hear the pre-ringing alone on a kick, plus all-pass and Pultec-style moves
Open the lab →

Spectral dynamics

Resonance Anatomy Lab

What a resonance suppressor like soothe detects, where it cuts, how much and how fast — with test signals that carry planted resonances you can check against.

  • Drag Selectivity, Depth, width and smoothing right on the graphs
  • Delta listen: hear only what is removed
  • Ten lessons in a floating panel and 13 practical recipes
Open the lab →

Metering

Meter Bridge

One signal, six meters — VU, Peak, True Peak, RMS, PPM and LUFS side by side, so you can see why the same “−18” means different things.

  • Live VU needles, bar meters and a 10-second history
  • Seven guided experiments with expected readings
  • Integrated, LRA, PLR and inter-sample peaks
Open the lab →

Delay · Basics

Delay Basics Lab

Slapback, doubling, Haas, ping-pong, dotted eighths, dub throws and short ambience — seven classic uses, heard and seen.

  • Echo timeline with beat grid and per-repeat filtering
  • Mono check, wet-only and delay-off A/B
  • Matching settings for Ableton Echo, Valhalla Delay and EchoBoy
Open the lab →

Delay · Machines

Analog Delay Lab

How tape slap, Echoplex, Space Echo, magnetic drum, oil can, BBD and early digital delays actually worked — and why they sound the way they do.

  • Animated diagrams of each mechanism
  • Frequency response of every repeat
  • Calculators: tape speed, BBD clock, memory and bit depth
